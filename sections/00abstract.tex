\begin{abstract}
	Small bioactivity data sets make it easy to overstate the benefit of complex models, through data leakage and through model selection that is rarely evaluated on unseen data. We re-examined this for inhibitors of \textit{Toxoplasma gondii} dihydrofolate reductase (TgDHFR, $N = \NCompounds$ curated compounds), comparing classical stacking ensembles on four molecular representations with a Directed Message Passing Neural Network (D-MPNN) on the same \TotalOuterFolds\ folds of a repeated nested cross-validation. Ensembles on Morgan fingerprints reached $R^2 = \MorganRTwoMean \pm \MorganRTwoStd$; adding 3D descriptors changed nothing detectable ($\Delta R^2 = \NBdiffRTwoTwoDFpVsTwoDThreeDFp$). The D-MPNN reached $R^2 = \ChemPropDMPNNFoldRTwoMean$ with default settings and $\GnnTunedRTwoMean$ after a nested hyperparameter search, trailing the fingerprint-based ensembles by a small margin that reached significance only narrowly after multiple-comparison correction (smallest adjusted $p = \GnnTunedVsMinPRTwo$ for $R^2$), and statistically indistinguishable from, and equivalent to, the 2D descriptors alone. Equivalence with the Morgan ensemble could not be established in either metric. Averaging five networks recovered part of the gap, and a D-MPNN initialized from a pretrained foundation model closed it ($R^2 = \ChemeleonRTwoMean$, equivalent to the Morgan ensemble in both metrics) at roughly eight times the computational cost; no model exceeded the fingerprint ensembles. Re-running the original study's published code and removing its information leaks one at a time lowered its reported $R^2$ from \KarLadderRungZeroRTwoMean\ to \KarLadderRungFourRTwoMean, the level of a model with no feature selection at all; the largest single contribution came from a feature importance computed on the test molecules. Feature selection and Gaussian-noise augmentation did not improve a classical learner either, and reproduced the reported gain only when the selection was allowed to see the test data. With scaffold-grouped splits every model lost between \ScaffoldChemeleonDropRTwo\ and \ScaffoldRdkitTwoDDropRTwo\ units of $R^2$, least of all the pretrained network. The classical benchmark was not cheaper to run than the network's. Applying the deployed model, a feature-selected Morgan-fingerprint ensemble, to an FDA-approved library (\ScreenNLibrary\ compounds) and docking \ScreenNCandidates\ candidates gave \AdmetNConsensus\ consensus hits, \AdmetNConsensusSurvivors\ of which passed in silico ADMET filters; potency--docking agreement was weak ($\rho = \DockSpearmanRho$) and selectivity over human DHFR was not assessed. For this target, a fixed-fingerprint ensemble was sufficient; we make no claim beyond it.
\end{abstract}
