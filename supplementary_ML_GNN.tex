\section*{Virtual screening and molecular docking}

\subsection*{Ligand efficiency metrics}

Four efficiency metrics contextualize a predicted potency against the physicochemical load that carries it, which
mitigates the risk of prioritizing compounds of high predicted potency and unfavourable properties for development
\citep{hopkins2004}. Ligand efficiency (LE) is the binding energy per heavy atom count (HAC) \citep{hopkins2014}:
\begin{equation}
	\text{LE} = \frac{1.37 \times pIC_{50}}{\text{HAC}}
\end{equation}
The factor of 1.37 converts a dissociation constant into a binding free energy, so substituting $pIC_{50}$ for $pK_d$
assumes the two are interchangeable, which holds only under the Cheng--Prusoff conditions \citep{cheng1973}. The
assumption is inherited from the data set, whose potencies are $IC_{50}$ values measured in assays whose substrate
concentrations are not reported, and it is one reason none of these metrics was used as a filter. The binding
efficiency index (BEI) substitutes the heavy-atom count with the molecular weight (MW) \citep{abad2005}:
\begin{equation}
	\text{BEI} = \frac{pIC_{50}}{\text{MW}} \times 1000
\end{equation}
Lipophilic ligand efficiency balances potency against lipophilicity, to mitigate the risk of nonspecific binding, as
the difference between predicted potency and the calculated octanol--water partition coefficient
($\text{LLE} = pIC_{50} - \log P$, Wildman--Crippen method in RDKit). The surface efficiency index evaluates potency
relative to the topological polar surface area (TPSA), as a proxy for membrane permeability
($\text{SEI} = (pIC_{50} / \text{TPSA}) \times 100$). Because composite indices combining logarithmic potency values
with linear physical parameters can introduce size-dependent mathematical artifacts \citep{shultz2013}, the four
metrics were computed from the predicted $pIC_{50}$ and reported alongside it for interpretation; candidates were
ranked and selected by predicted potency alone.

\subsection*{Docking protocol}

The structure-based check complements the ligand-based predictions by assessing complementarity with the binding site, which mitigates the risk of false positives produced by scaffold memorization. AutoDock Vina v1.2.7 \citep{rajesh2026, trott2009} employs an Iterated Local Search optimizer and a hybrid scoring function, calibrated against PDBbind data, to guide flexible ligands into optimal binding conformations \citep{eberhardt2021}.

Three-dimensional receptor coordinates were retrieved from the Protein Data Bank (\textit{Toxoplasma gondii} DHFR, PDB ID: 6AOG) \citep{consortium2019, berman2003}. The receptor was prepared as a rigid apo structure: all non-protein records (crystallographic waters, ions, cofactors including the bound NADPH, and the co-crystallized ligand) were removed, retaining both protein chains. Hydrogens were added at pH 7.4 and the structure was converted to the PDBQT format with OpenBabel in rigid mode \citep{babel2011}; keeping the polar hydrogens types the backbone N--H groups as hydrogen-bond donors, which the Vina scoring function distinguishes from acceptors (the partial charges written to the file are not used by this scoring function). While excluding the NADPH cofactor omits a potential source of active-site polarization, this apo-receptor approximation is a common simplification in structure-based virtual screening. The Vina search space was centered on the coordinates of the native \RedockLigand{} ligand in chain \RedockChain{} ($x = \DockCenterX{}, y = \DockCenterY{}, z = \DockCenterZ$) with a cubic grid box of $\DockBoxSize{} \times \DockBoxSize{} \times \DockBoxSize{}\text{ \AA}$ and an exhaustiveness parameter of \DockExhaustiveness{}, which permitted unrestricted exploration of ligand orientations and rotatable bonds within the site \citep{trott2009}.

Prior to screening the machine learning candidates, the docking protocol was checked by re-docking the co-crystallized \RedockLigand{} ligand into the TgDHFR active site. Because the PDB file of the ligand has no bond orders, they were assigned from the known structure of pyrimethamine before the docking input was built, and the starting conformer was generated independently of the crystallographic pose. The top-ranked pose reproduced the crystallographic pose with a symmetry-aware heavy-atom RMSD of $\RedockRMSD\text{ \AA}$ (centroid distance $\RedockCentroidDist\text{ \AA}$), below the $\RedockThreshold\text{ \AA}$ criterion adopted for validation. The search box was centered on the crystallographic ligand, so the check confirms that the grid parameters and the docking setup are consistent with the known binding site, but it does not demonstrate blind pose prediction, and it was run on a single ligand.

Following validation, Vina evaluated all \DockNTotal{} candidates of the screening step (the 10 top-ranked compounds plus the reference entries). For each compound the largest fragment was retained to remove counter-ions, hydrogens were added, a single 3D conformer was generated with ETKDGv3 (fixed seed) and minimized with MMFF, and a flexible-ligand PDBQT file was prepared with Meeko, keeping the protonation state of the input structure. Docking used a fixed random seed and returned the \DockNPoses\ best poses. Poses were ranked by the predicted binding free energy ($\Delta G$) in kcal/mol, where lower energies denote stronger predicted binding \citep{feng2023}; this is an empirical, rigid-receptor estimate and not a measured thermodynamic quantity. The docking ligand efficiency was computed as the best score per heavy atom. To systematically evaluate the consensus between the ligand-based and structure-based approaches, we established quantitative thresholds. Compounds were classified as validated consensus hits only if they exhibited both high ML-predicted potency ($pIC_{50} > \DockMLActiveThresh$) and strong docking affinity ($\Delta G < \DockActiveThresh\text{ kcal/mol}$). Compounds meeting the ML threshold but failing the docking criterion were flagged as potential ML false positives. Conversely, compounds with $pIC_{50} < \DockMLInactiveThresh$ were labeled validated negatives if $\Delta G > \DockInactiveThresh\text{ kcal/mol}$ and potential ML false negatives if $\Delta G < \DockActiveThresh\text{ kcal/mol}$; all remaining compounds were classed as neutral. Finally, to quantify the monotonic agreement between the two orthogonal methods without assuming linearity, a Spearman rank correlation analysis was conducted between the continuous $pIC_{50}$ predictions and the Vina docking scores (reported in the manuscript).
